Game night ideas with one TV

Updated 23 November 2026

We make Joypad Party, and about half of these ideas use it. The rest need nothing but the people in the room.

With one TV, pick games where everyone plays at the same time on their own phone, so nobody waits for a controller. For 2–4 people play head-to-head or co-op games, for 5–10 a quiz or a drawing game, and for 10–20 a voting or drawing game; then keep the rounds short and save one game for a final.

The one-TV problem

A TV game night usually stalls in the same place: one remote, maybe two gamepads, and eight people. Two play, six watch, and the watchers drift off to the kitchen. Taking turns works for a while, until the turns get long and the person at the end of the queue gives up.

Three things fix it:

  • A controller for everyone. Phone-controller games turn every phone in the room into that game's controller, so twelve people play as easily as two.
  • Games sized to the group. A two-player racer with ten guests is a queue, not a party. Pick by group size first, below.
  • Short rounds. Ten-minute games let latecomers join the next one and let the evening change pace before anyone gets bored.

In Joypad Party, one room holds up to 20 players. If a game seats fewer people than are connected, the extra phones show a Watching page that tells them they're in the next game, so nobody has to leave the room and rejoin. Every way of playing together on one screen, gamepads included, is in local multiplayer on Apple TV.

Ideas by group size

Each size gets an idea on the TV and one with no screen at all, and most get one that mixes the two. Not everything is ours, and a good game night doesn't need an app for every round.

2 people

  • On the TV: Hexcast, best of five, where the loser of each round picks the next start level. It runs on every Apple TV that runs Joypad Party, and one of you can play with the Siri Remote (tested in Apple's tvOS Simulator so far; a real Apple TV test is next).
  • No screen: a pack of cards and a game you both already know, for the minutes when one of you is making drinks.
  • Mix: a Showquiz duel where each of you picks a topic for the other. More for two in 2-player games on Apple TV.

3 or 4 people

  • On the TV: Tumbledown (1–4), a tin-toy brawler on a spinning record. The computer fills empty seats, so three players still get a full ring. It needs an Apple TV 4K (2nd generation or later).
  • No screen: charades, with a phone as the timer and a bowl of film titles everyone wrote down when they arrived.
  • Mix: Talkdown (2–4). The TV shows a time machine, each phone holds a different part of it, and the only way through is talking to each other. Lamplight Canteen (1–4) is the calmer co-op choice. Both need tvOS 26 on an Apple TV 4K (2nd generation or later).

5 to 10 people

  • On the TV: Showquiz for up to 16 players, or Drawshow (3–16), where one player draws on their phone, the picture appears live on the TV, and everyone else types guesses.
  • No screen: Two Truths and a Lie. Everyone says three things about themselves, one of them false, and the room votes on which.
  • Mix: Snowbluff (3–16). Every phone shows the same secret word except the crasher's, and the questions are asked out loud across the room. It needs tvOS 26 on an Apple TV 4K (2nd generation or later).

10 to 20 people

  • On the TV: Pickoff (1–20) or Scribblegram (3–20). Pickoff is the easy opener: the room votes on each match-up until one favourite is left. Scribblegram is the big laugh: sentences become drawings become sentences, and the TV plays every chain back.
  • No screen: Werewolf with paper slips and a volunteer narrator.

More than 20 guests? Pair people up on one phone in Showquiz, Pickoff or Scribblegram and play as teams of two. Couples tend to argue about the answer anyway.

The setup checklist

Do these before the doorbell goes:

  1. Get the app onto everyone's phone in advance. Send the App Store link to the group chat the day before. Anyone who arrives without it can point their iPhone camera at the QR code on the TV's connect screen, which opens the App Store page.
  2. Put the Wi-Fi name and password where people can see it. The phones and the Apple TV must be on the same network, and guest networks and some routers keep devices apart.
  3. Check that one phone connects, ten minutes before. When iOS asks whether the app may find devices on your local network, tap Allow (Apple explains the prompt).
  4. Charge the phones and the Siri Remote. During party games the app keeps each phone's screen on so nobody drops out between turns. That uses battery, so leave a charging cable by the sofa.
  5. Turn the TV sound up. The countdowns keep the pace.
  6. Decide who hosts. The first phone to join is the host, and it picks the games and the settings. Give that job to the person who'll still be paying attention at the end of the night.
  7. Have a one-sentence rule for each game. Read it out before the first round:
    • Showquiz: answer A to D on your phone before the 20 seconds run out.
    • Snowbluff: everyone sees the same word except the crasher, so find the crasher.
    • Scribblegram: draw what you got, describe what you see.

Keeping score across games

A running total gives the night a winner without anyone taking it too seriously:

  • Showquiz: the podium's top three score 3, 2 and 1.
  • Scribblegram: one point for every award the TV hands out at the end, such as Masterpiece or Most laughs.
  • Snowbluff: the game keeps its own points; its top three score 3, 2 and 1.
  • Tumbledown: 2 points for each match won.
  • A tie at the end: one Showquiz show in Survival mode between the tied players. Three lives each, last one standing takes the night.

When people arrive late or drop out

Game nights don't start on time, and phones ring. Here's what happens in Joypad Party, so the host doesn't have to restart anything:

  • Late arrivals open the app on the same Wi-Fi and join the room. A phone that joins mid-game plays from the next question in Showquiz, votes from the next match-up in Pickoff, gets an invitation from the next round in Snowbluff, and joins Scribblegram at the next game.
  • A phone drops out, say someone takes a call in the hallway: the party games carry on without it, and opening Joypad Party again reconnects the phone to the room. In Hexcast and Showquiz, the TV may pause and wait for that player.
  • Someone leaves early: they tap ••• at the top of their phone, then Leave session, and the room carries on without them.

Food, seats and the rest

Keep it simple. Food you can eat with one hand, because the other holds a phone. Napkins by the sofa, because greasy fingers and phones don't mix. Seats where everyone can read the TV: the questions and the drawings are on the big screen, not on the phones.

Questions

Which games can a group of 10 or more play?

In Joypad Party: Scribblegram (3–20), Pickoff (1–20), Showquiz (1–16), Drawshow (3–16) and Snowbluff (3–16). Each player needs their own iPhone or iPad with the app.

Do I need an internet connection to play?

Not to play. The phones and the TV talk over your home Wi-Fi, so they must be on the same network. You need the internet to download the app, and Final Watch downloads extra content the first time you open it.

How many people can play?

It depends on the game. Hexcast takes 1–2 players; Latenight Streets, Final Watch, Tumbledown, Lamplight Canteen and Boardwalk Bits 1–4; Talkdown 2–4; Haute Coop 2–8; Showquiz 1–16; Drawshow and Snowbluff 3–16; Pickoff 1–20; Scribblegram 3–20. Everyone plays at once, each on their own phone.
